Step 1 — load an image. Step 2 — click (or tap) the outer face of the first ring, then the outer face of the last ring, to set the axis. Both ends count as boundaries, so tap precisely — pinch-zoom your browser or steady your elbows first if the stack is small on screen. Tip: a ring boundary is often where the surface finish changes — one face catching more glare (specular/shiny) than its neighbour (matte/diffuse) — even when the two faces are nearly the same brightness. The Detection filter below lets you process on that shine difference instead of plain brightness. If rings are thin and tightly packed, start with a low Min. gap.
📱 Shooting the photo: hold the phone square to the stack's axis (tilt = a real cosine error, not a pixel one) and rest your elbows or the phone against something solid — a slightly blurred shot costs more accuracy than any setting below. Tap-to-focus directly on the stack, use 1× or 2× rather than a heavy digital zoom, and keep the whole stack filling as much of the frame as you can.
Profile: grey = active filter channel along the axis (brightness or shine, per Detection filter above), teal = detected boundary strength. Click a boundary line on the image to delete it; click elsewhere on the axis to add one manually.
